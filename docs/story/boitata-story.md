# O Fogo que Guarda: the demo as a story

A folheto de cordel told as a shadow play. Nine short chapters, each one a scene of the library; between them the story pauses and the scene is handed to the viewer ("Mexa na cena"), with its parameters laid out as a carved control panel and a "ficha técnica" that explains the physics behind them.

The whole thing is a **cordel**: the woodcut art is the cordel's *xilogravura*, the narration is in short rhymed verses (draft verses below, rewrite to taste), the booklet cover is the menu.

## The frame: a shadow play at the São João festival

It is the night of São João (24 June: winter in the south, cold nights, hence the bonfire, the hot *quentão*, the frost). In a clearing, children sit before a paper screen with a lamp behind it, and someone tells the legend of Boitatá with cut-out puppets. We begin in that shadow play; when the story gets serious the paper catches fire from the Boitatá's own flame, burns away (paper burning to flying ash) and the "real" forest appears behind it. At the end we go back to the festival, to the screen and the lamp, and blow it out.

*Why this works:* it opens with the optics (a lamp behind paper, soft glow, shadows that grow near the lamp), it justifies the cordel style, it makes the festival (lanterns, colours, bonfire) the place where the story is told, and it gives a natural way to end.

*A note on the geography:* the legend of the Boitatá is Tupi-Guarani, from the south and the forests; Lampião and Maria Bonita are of the sertão, in the northeast. The narrator says the story "travelled with the tropeiros": a cordel is allowed to walk from the araucária forest to the caatinga. Lampião and Maria Bonita are the folk cordel versions, not the historical people.

### Draft verses (to rewrite; one per chapter, shown as a cordel ribbon with a translation)

## The theme, so it holds together

**Fogo bom e fogo mau** (good fire and bad fire). The bonfire of São João, the lamp, the coffee on the stove, the lantern: fire that warms, lights and is shared. The hunter's fire: fire that destroys. The Boitatá is not against fire, it is against *that* fire. Every chapter is a way of seeing fire: as light and shadow (0, 1, 4), as what it spares and what it takes (2, 3), as what it does to people and water (5, 6, 7), and as warmth and its end (8).

## Using every feature

- **Light and shadow:** theatre (0), forest light from the serpent (1), the light stage with coloured lamps, walls, shadows across people (4), the flash of the shot on the house wall (7), the fire's glitter on the pond (6).
- **Optics and glass:** paper screen (0), stained and old glass, window light, dirty glass (4), frost and fog on the pane (4, 8).
- **Weather on surfaces:** the fogged window with rain beads and the wiped heart (4), frost and snow on the sill (8).
- **Fire:** guardian vs ordinary (2), spreading with the wind and all the materials (3), the burning person (5, 7), the burning kerosene (6), the paper screen burning away (1), balloons and bunting that can catch (4).
- **Liquids:** coffee and quentão on the table, steam, boil, foam, viscosity and density, knocked over, spill, puddle, drips off the table edge (4, 8); the kerosene spill (6); the pond (6).
- **Wind:** the trees, leaves, paina, grass, bananeira, cipó, flags and the balloon (1, 4), the ash of the hunter (7).
- **Depth and parallax:** the forest (1), the chase (5), dragging characters in the pauses.
- **Sky and sound:** the storm with thunder (0), moon phases and stars (1, 4), dawn (8), ambience (rain, wind, fire crackle); the gunshot (7) and a forró loop (4) are new.
- **Performance:** quality settings in every pause.

## What the story needs that the library does not have yet

1. **A story director:** a timeline of events by time with named pause points, subtitles (the cordel ribbon), transitions, and a scripted camera (pan, push in). The playground scripts a few things by hand already; this makes it a system.
2. **The paper burning away as a transition** (the paper material and the burn exist; the transition is the work).
3. **Characters that move:** walk, run and dance cycles for Lampião, Maria and the hunter (art), the hunter running *while burning*, animals that flee from fire (a small behaviour).
4. **The gunshot:** muzzle flash, smoke, a fast bullet, and a synthesised shot sound (the audio synth has thunder, rain, wind and fire).
5. **Festival things:** wind-driven bunting (a chain of flags), paper balloons that rise and can catch fire, fireworks that flash coloured light on everything (using the multi-light rig), a bonfire people jump over.
6. **The parameter panels** (below).

## The control panels: "Mexa na cena"

At each pause a drawer rises from the bottom of the screen, styled as a cordel booklet page: ink on warm paper, a carved border, and controls made of woodcut parts.

- **Dials and sliders**, carved: a rod with a notched knob; round dials with ticks for angles (wind direction, hour, moon).
- **Chips as stamps**: the options (liquids, glass types, weather) are inked stamps that press in when chosen.
- **Readouts as instruments**: a thermometer, a wind rose with the gust as a needle, a moon dial that shows the phase, a dew-point gauge that turns misty, a Beaufort scale.
- **The ficha técnica:** a flap that opens on each panel with the one-line physics of what you are moving (for example "Dew point 9.3 degrees: glass colder than this fogs", "Tipping angle 31 degrees: knock it past this and it falls", "Reflectance 6% at this angle, drawn at 42%"), each as a small woodcut card.
- **Language:** Portuguese and English, switched with a button.

The chapter index is the booklet's cover ("folheto"): a list of chapters as bookmarks with a progress ribbon, and a "Oficina" door at the end that opens every tab freely.
